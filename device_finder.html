How to find your device

  1. Follow your device. The laptop then "calls" its Bluetooth address about once a second. A phone answers whenever its Bluetooth is ON – screen off, in a pocket, connected to earbuds: all fine. It does not need to be visible.
    Follow a different address

    A phone shows its Bluetooth address in Settings → About phone → Status. The meter also remembers every phone it has seen.

  2. Walk slowly and pause 2–3 seconds at each spot. The big number is then the phone's connection signal: above 0 (like +5) = right here · 0 = within a few metres · −1 to −9 = same room · −10 to −20 = next room · below −20 = far away. Up close it changes only a little – Bluetooth can't measure finer there, so use your eyes and ears for the last metres. With sound on, faster beeps mean closer.
  3. "OUT OF REACH" everywhere? Then its Bluetooth is off, or it is more than ~10 m away. Press "Play sound" here (it rings for 5 minutes, even on silent). The LE listener below still catches phones that wake up:
    New signals get a NEW badge and a "ding".
Trick: make a silent phone show up (Quick Share)

Install Google's Quick Share app for Windows on this laptop. Sign in with the same Google account as your phone, then start sending any small file. Google says phones set to "Your devices" stay visible to their own account even with the screen off. If your phone appears in Quick Share's list, it is nearby and switched on, and it may show up here as a NEW "Google Nearby" signal.

Following only works when the phone's Bluetooth is ON. If it is off, the phone sends nothing at all, and no laptop can hear it – then only "Play sound" or a Wi-Fi check can help.
